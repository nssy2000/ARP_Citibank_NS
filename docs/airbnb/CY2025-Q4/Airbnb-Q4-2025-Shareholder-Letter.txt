Q4 2025 Shareholder Letter

Airbnb closed out 2025 with strong momentum, delivering double-digit growth across key top-line metrics in Q4. Despite a tough YoY comparison, the business accelerated in Q4 vs Q3. Revenue grew 12%, exceeding the high end of guidance; GBV grew 16% YoY -- the highest-growth quarter in more than two years; Nights and Seats Booked rose 10%, the strongest growth quarter of the year, driven by demand strength across all regions and ADR.

Making Airbnb better: Reserve Now, Pay Later (US $0-upfront reservation feature) contributed to the acceleration of bookings in Q4; testing completed across additional markets in February 2026, with plans for broader global availability in 2026. New cancellation policies announced in October (free cancellation up to 14 days before check-in under a new Limited policy; 24-hour grace period for refunds on shorter stays) reduced customer service contacts and increased bookings.

Bringing Airbnb to more parts of the world: operating in 220+ countries/regions but majority of business concentrated in five countries. Origin nights booked in expansion markets grew at roughly twice the rate of core markets in 2025. Particularly strong Q4 performance in Brazil and Japan; India became one of the fastest-growing countries on an origin basis, nights booked up 50% YoY, first-time bookers up more than 60% YoY (all three countries saw double-digit first-time-booker growth).

Expanding what we offer: since May launch of Airbnb Services and Airbnb Experiences, onboarded significantly more hosts in H2 2025; piloting new services with partners including grocery delivery. In Q4, almost half of experiences bookings were not attached to an accommodation booking. Began partnering directly with boutique/independent hotels in New York, Los Angeles, Madrid, and San Francisco (supply-constrained cities), with plans to expand to other key markets.

Integrating AI: AI-powered customer support rolled out for English, French and Spanish speakers across the US, Canada and Mexico -- about a third of all issues resolved without needing an agent, with significant reduction in resolution time; plan to roll out globally later in 2026. AI-powered search in early testing, focused on natural-language trip descriptions.

Outlook
2026 Q1: Revenue expected $2.59B-$2.63B, YoY growth 14%-16% (~3-point FX tailwind). Implied take rate expected up slightly YoY. GBV expected to increase low teens YoY (high-single-digit Nights/Seats Booked growth + moderate ADR increase). Adjusted EBITDA Margin expected approximately flat YoY.
Full-Year 2026: revenue growth expected to accelerate to at least low double digits, driven by sustained strength in core business, healthy demand, and continued execution on growth initiatives. Adjusted EBITDA Margin expected stable YoY as top-line efficiencies are reinvested in marketing, product, and technology.

Reserve Now, Pay Later: over 70% adoption by eligible bookings (based on global GBV in Q4 2025) after strong US launch; rolling out to more guests in 2026.

Q4 2025 / FY2025 Business and Financial Performance
Q4: Nights & Seats Booked 121.9M (10% Y/Y), GBV $20.4B (16% Y/Y, 13% ex-FX). FY: Nights & Seats Booked 533.0M (8% Y/Y), GBV $91.3B (12% Y/Y, 10% ex-FX). Nights and Seats Booked growth was the highest YoY rate of any 2025 quarter. App nights booked +20% YoY, 64% of total nights booked (up from 60% prior year). First-time bookers growth accelerated to 8% YoY in Q4, strong across all age cohorts. ADR $168 in Q4 (+6% YoY, +3% ex-FX), up across all regions on price appreciation.

Geographic Performance:
- North America: mid-single digit growth in Nights/Seats Booked YoY, driven by domestic travel and longer lead times; ADR +5% YoY (mix shift, especially 4+ bedroom listings).
- EMEA: high-single digit growth YoY (acceleration vs Q3), broad demand improvement across core and expansion markets including UK, Spain, Italy; ADR +12% YoY (+4% ex-FX).
- Latin America: high-teens growth YoY; ADR +9% YoY (+3% ex-FX). Brazil origin nights booked +20%+, first-time bookers +17% YoY; Mexico origin nights booked accelerated to up high-teens YoY.
- Asia Pacific: mid-teens growth YoY; ADR +2% YoY (+2% ex-FX). Continued strength in Japan domestic travel; India origin nights booked +50% YoY, first-time bookers +60%+ YoY.

Supply: ended 2025 with over 9 million active listings; Q4 active listings grew roughly in-line with Nights/Seats Booked growth, most growth in Latin America and Asia Pacific. Since 2023 hosting-quality system launch, removed over 550,000 listings; YoY reduction in customer service issue rates and chargeback rates during 2025. Over 500 million nights booked at Guest Favorite listings since launch.

Q4 2025 / FY2025 Financial Detail
Q4 Revenue $2.8B (+12% YoY, driven by strong nights-stayed growth and moderate ADR increase). Q4 Net Income $341M, lower than Q4 2024 as higher revenue was offset by planned growth/policy investments and ~$90 million of non-income tax matters (net income margin 12% vs 19% in Q4 2024). FY2025 effective tax rate 20%, including a one-time $213 million CAMT valuation allowance offset partly by a $60 million uncertain-tax-position release and FDII benefit. Starting 2026, long-term effective tax rate expected to decline to mid-to-high teens due to OBBBA (enacted July 4, 2025). Q4 Adjusted EBITDA $786M (+3% YoY), 28% margin (vs 31% in Q4 2024).

Monetization and Take Rate: implied take rate 13.6% in Q4, down YoY from 14.1%, primarily FX and booking/stay timing. FY2025 guest travel insurance revenue (available in 12 largest countries) up ~40% YoY. Since October, simplifying fee structure: PMS hosts migrated from split 3%-host-fee structure to single 15.5% service fee; most non-PMS single-fee hosts also moved to 15.5% as of December.

Stock-Based Compensation: FY2025 SBC expense increased 13% YoY, driven by headcount growth; 2026 SBC and headcount growth rate expected lower than 2025.

Shares: repurchased $1.1 billion of Class A common stock during Q4 2025, continuing reduction in total common shares outstanding.

Balance Sheet and Cash Flows: as of December 31, 2025, $11.0 billion of cash and cash equivalents, short-term investments, and restricted cash, plus $7.0 billion of funds held on behalf of guests. $5.6 billion remained under the $6 billion share repurchase program as of December 31, 2025. Since inception of repurchases in Q3 2022, fully diluted share count decreased ~9%; repurchases plus employee tax obligations total $13.6 billion.
