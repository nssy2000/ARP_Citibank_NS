Q1 2026 Shareholder Letter

Airbnb had a strong start to 2026. Revenue grew 18% year-over-year to $2.7 billion, exceeding the high end of guidance. Guests spent nearly $30 billion on Airbnb in Q1. Gross Booking Value grew 19% year-over-year, driven by strong demand and continued pricing strength, aided by an FX tailwind. Nights and Seats Booked grew 9%, reflecting healthy underlying demand. Net income was $160 million and Adjusted EBITDA was $519 million, up 24% year-over-year.

Nights booked on the app accelerated during Q1, growing 22% year-over-year and accounting for 63% of total nights booked, up from 58% a year ago. First-time booker growth accelerated to 10% -- the highest growth since early 2022 -- with particularly strong acceleration in Brazil, Japan, and India. Origin net nights booked in expansion markets grew at roughly twice the rate of core markets. In India, origin nights booked grew approximately 50% year-over-year; in Brazil, origin nights grew over 20% for the third consecutive quarter.

The company is navigating macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty: slightly elevated cancellations this quarter in EMEA and Asia Pacific, primarily driven by the conflict in the Middle East.

Making Airbnb better for hosts and guests: roughly 20% of global GBV came from Reserve Now, Pay Later bookings in Q1. Improving pricing tools for dynamic pricing based on demand/seasonality and redesigned host sign-up flow.

Expanding what Airbnb offers: continued piloting new categories of services and experiences in select cities, plans to expand both this summer. Nearly a quarter of guests new to Airbnb who book an experience go on to book a stay or service; roughly one in three experience bookers book a stay within 90 days. Expanding partnership with Delta Air Lines to allow travelers to earn Delta miles on qualifying Airbnb Experiences and Services (in addition to homes). Scaling boutique/independent hotel pilot to more markets; approximately 55% of guests who book a hotel on Airbnb come back to book a home.

Major events: Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games Milano Cortina 2026 -- nearly 200,000 guests stayed with Airbnb, supply in host markets grew approximately 30%, ~1 billion impressions from marketing campaigns. FIFA World Cup 2026 (this summer) expected to be the largest event in Airbnb's history; over 100,000 homes across the 16 World Cup host cities listed on Airbnb for the first time since outreach began in October.

Leveraging AI: nearly 60% of engineers' code now coauthored with AI (roughly twice the industry average, per company estimate). For guests contacting support through the AI Assistant, over 40% of issues now resolved without a human agent (up from roughly a third in Q4 2025), with faster resolution times. Cost-per-booking decreased about 10% year-over-year in Q1.

Outlook
Q2 2026: Revenue expected $3.54B-$3.60B, YoY growth 14%-16% (~3% FX tailwind). Implied take rate expected up slightly YoY. GBV expected to increase low double digits YoY (Nights/Seats Booked growth + moderate ADR increase, smaller FX tailwind than Q1). Nights and Seats Booked growth expected to slightly decelerate vs Q1 2026 (~100bps headwind estimated from Middle East conflict). Adjusted EBITDA and margin expected up YoY.
Full-Year 2026: raising guidance -- revenue growth now expected to accelerate to low-to-mid teens YoY. Adjusted EBITDA Margin now expected at least 35%. Continuing to prioritize reinvestment in efficient marketing spend, international expansion, and AI initiatives.

The upward revision to the revenue outlook reflects progress across growth initiatives and monetization improvements (simplified fee structure, insurance programs) expected to lift the full-year take rate. Tougher comparisons expected in H2 2026 against 2025's Reserve Now, Pay Later rollout and current headwinds from the Middle East conflict.

Q1 2026 Business and Financial Performance
Nights & Seats Booked 156.2M (9% Y/Y) despite increased cancellations from the Middle East conflict; absent that impact, growth would have been approximately 10% YoY (an acceleration vs Q1 2025). Lengthening of lead times across all regions, partly from Reserve Now, Pay Later expansion. App nights booked +22% YoY, 63% of total (up from 58% prior year). First-time bookers growth accelerated to 10% YoY (highest since early 2022), particularly strong in Brazil, Japan, India. GBV $29.2B (+19% YoY, +13% ex-FX). ADR $187 (+9% YoY, +4% ex-FX), up across all regions on price appreciation.

Geographic Performance:
- North America: high-single digit growth in Nights and Seats Booked YoY (modest acceleration vs Q4 2025), driven by domestic travel and longer lead times. ADR +7% YoY (mix shift + price appreciation).
- EMEA: mid-single digit growth YoY. Nearly 200,000 guests stayed on Airbnb and supply grew ~30% in the Milan region for the Olympic Games. Increase in cancellations across EMEA from the Middle East conflict slightly weighed on growth. ADR +15% YoY (+4% ex-FX).
- Latin America: high-teens growth YoY. ADR +10% YoY (+3% ex-FX). Brazil origin nights booked +20%+ for the third consecutive quarter, first-time bookers +22% YoY. Continued double-digit nights growth in Mexico.
- Asia Pacific: high-teens growth YoY. ADR +6% YoY (+2% ex-FX). India origin nights booked ~50% YoY for the second consecutive quarter, first-time bookers accelerated to over 75% YoY.

Supply: active listings grew relatively in-line with Nights and Seats Booked growth, most growth in Latin America and Asia Pacific. Supply strategy focused on the right homes in the right place, concentrating acquisition on markets with underlying demand while encouraging existing hosts to open more calendar availability.

Q1 2026 Financial Detail
Revenue $2.7B (+18% YoY, driven by strong nights-stayed growth and meaningful ADR increase aided by FX tailwind). Net income $160 million, up slightly vs Q1 2025, driven by higher revenue, a one-time ~$70 million gain on sale of a privately-held equity investment, and operating expense efficiencies (including customer support), offset by continued sales/marketing investment and a one-time ~$70 million deferred tax asset adjustment from changes to the U.S. Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax effective in Q1. Net income margin 6% (vs 7% in Q1 2025). Adjusted EBITDA $519 million (+24% YoY vs $417 million in Q1 2025), 19% margin (vs 18% in Q1 2025). Full-year 2026 effective tax rate expected in the high teens.

Monetization and Take Rate: implied take rate 9.2%, relatively in-line with 9.3% in Q1 2025 (FX and booking/stay timing effects reflecting Reserve Now, Pay Later driving guests to book further in advance). Guest travel insurance revenue (available in 12 largest countries) up 45% YoY; piloting additional insurance products.

Since Q4 2025, simplifying fee structure: migrating PMS hosts from split fee structure (3% host fee + separate guest service fee) to a single 15.5% service fee for most hosts; testing expansion of this structure to other countries.

Shares: repurchased $1.1 billion of Class A common stock during Q1 2026. Fully diluted share count reduced from 677M (Mar 2024 baseline shown) to 641M (Mar 2026).

Balance Sheet and Cash Flows: as of March 31, 2026, $12.1 billion of cash and cash equivalents, short-term investments, and restricted cash, plus $10.6 billion of funds held on behalf of guests; $2.7 billion of unearned fees (stable YoY). Net cash provided by operating activities and FCF were both $1.7 billion for the quarter; FCF declined slightly YoY due to working-capital impact from continued Reserve Now, Pay Later expansion (which shifts payment timing closer to date of stay, lowering unearned fees in Q1/Q2 and raising them in Q3). In Q1 2026, received inaugural investment-grade ratings of A- from S&P Global Ratings and Baa1 from Moody's, completed a $2.5 billion senior unsecured debt offering, and used proceeds to fully repay the $2.0 billion of convertible senior notes that matured in March 2026.

Capital Allocation: $1.1 billion of Class A common stock repurchased in Q1 2026; $4.5 billion remained under repurchase authorization as of March 31, 2026. Since inception of repurchases in Q3 2022, fully diluted share count decreased ~9%; repurchases plus employee tax obligations total $14.8 billion.
